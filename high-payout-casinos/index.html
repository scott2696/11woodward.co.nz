What "high payout" actually means

Every pokie and table game has a return-to-player figure, written as RTP. It's the percentage of all money wagered that the game pays back to players over the long run. A pokie with a 96.5% RTP returns $96.50 for every $100 staked across millions of spins. The other $3.50 is the house edge — the casino's share.

That figure is a long-run average, not a promise for your session. Over an evening you might double your deposit or lose it entirely; RTP only smooths out over hundreds of thousands of spins. What it does tell you is which games are built to grind your bankroll down slowly versus quickly, and that matters when you're stretching a $50 NZD balance.

A genuine high payout casino does three things: it stocks pokies with published RTPs of 96% and up, it doesn't quietly run the low-RTP configurable versions of those games, and it returns your winnings fast in New Zealand dollars. A casino advertising a huge bonus while hiding its RTP figures is not a high payout casino — it's a high-marketing one.

RTP and house edge: two sides of the same coin

Return-to-player and house edge are the same number seen from opposite ends. If a pokie has a 96% RTP, the house edge is the remaining 4% — the built-in margin the casino keeps over the long run. Every real money casino game carries one; it's how the operator turns a profit while still paying winners. Understanding the payout rate is really just understanding how big that margin is on the game in front of you.

The maths is worth spelling out because it's routinely misread. RTP is a theoretical figure calculated over the full cycle of a game — often hundreds of millions of simulated spins. It is not a schedule of what you personally will win. Two players can sit at the same 96.5% pokie for an hour and one walks away up $200 while the other is down their whole $80 deposit. Both outcomes are entirely consistent with the same RTP, because the percentage only describes the average once the sample size becomes enormous.

What RTP tells you

The long-run share of stakes a game returns. Higher RTP means a smaller house edge and a bankroll that, on average, drains more slowly. A useful way to compare games before you play.

What RTP does not tell you

Your result tonight. It says nothing about a single session, the order wins arrive in, or how likely you are to leave ahead. That's decided by variance and plain luck.

Why the house always wins

Because the edge applies to every wager you make. The more spins you play, the closer your real return creeps to the theoretical RTP — which is always below 100%. Time favours the house, not you.

There's a second reason payout rates matter in New Zealand specifically: many pokies ship in more than one RTP "build". A studio like Pragmatic Play or Play'n GO may licence the same title at 96.5%, 94% and 92% settings, and the operator chooses which one to run. A high payout casino runs the top build; a margin-hungry one quietly loads the 92% version. Because the game looks identical, the only way to know is to open the info screen and read the number the casino is actually serving you.

Volatility and variance explained

Volatility — also called variance — is the missing half of the payout picture. Where RTP tells you how much a game returns over time, volatility tells you how that return is delivered: in a steady trickle of small wins, or in rare, large ones separated by long droughts. Two pokies can share an identical 96% RTP and feel like completely different games because their variance differs.

Low volatility

Frequent small wins, shallow swings. Your balance ticks up and down gently. Best for a modest NZD bankroll, long sessions, or clearing bonus wagering steadily. Blood Suckers and Starburst sit here.

Medium volatility

A balance of frequency and size. Wins come often enough to keep a session going, with the occasional bigger hit. Gonzo's Quest is a classic medium-variance title.

High volatility

Long losing runs punctuated by rare large payouts. You can burn through a deposit fast, but the top-end wins are far bigger. Megaways and most jackpot pokies live here.

Why this matters for a high payout hunter: a high RTP on a high-volatility pokie does not mean a comfortable session. You could play a 96.5% game for an hour and see almost nothing, because the return is concentrated in a bonus round you may never trigger. If you want your money to last and your wins to feel regular, pair a high RTP with low or medium volatility. If you're deliberately chasing one big result, high volatility is the trade you're making — just size your stakes so a dry run doesn't clear your balance in ten minutes.

RTP versus a jackpot — they are not the same thing

This trips up a lot of players. A jackpot is a single large prize; RTP is the average return of the game. They often pull in opposite directions.

Progressive jackpot pokies like Mega Moolah are famous for life-changing payouts, but their base-game RTP is usually lower — often around 88% to 94% — because a slice of every spin is siphoned off to build the jackpot pool. That money is technically part of the return, but almost all of it goes to the one player who eventually hits the jackpot. For everyone else, the effective return during normal play is lower than a plain 96% pokie.

So the choice is about what you want. If you're happy to accept a slightly worse day-to-day return for a tiny shot at a huge prize, a progressive jackpot pokie makes sense. If you want your bankroll to last and your wins to come more evenly, a high-RTP non-jackpot pokie is the better maths. A "high payout casino" for a jackpot chaser and for a grinder are genuinely different lists — decide which you are before you deposit.

Independent audits: how RTP is verified

An RTP figure is only worth trusting if something independent stands behind it. Reputable studios and casinos submit their games and their random number generators to third-party testing labs, which run millions of simulated rounds to confirm the game returns what it claims and that outcomes are genuinely random. When you see one of these bodies named, the payout rate has been checked by someone with no stake in the result.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good RTP for a high payout pokie?expand_more
For pokies, 96% or higher is considered good and 97%+ is excellent. RTP is the long-run percentage of stakes a game returns. A 96% pokie returns $96 for every $100 wagered across millions of spins — it says nothing about a single session, where results swing widely in both directions.
How is RTP different from a jackpot?expand_more
RTP is the average long-run return of a game; a jackpot is one large prize. Progressive jackpot pokies often have a lower base RTP because a slice of every spin feeds the jackpot pool. A high-RTP pokie without a jackpot usually gives steadier, more frequent returns than a jackpot chaser.
Do high payout casinos actually pay more?expand_more
A high payout casino stocks high-RTP games, publishes those figures, and cashes out quickly in NZD. The house still holds an edge on every game. High RTP improves your long-run odds slightly, but no casino or pokie is designed for the player to profit.
Can I check a pokie's RTP before I play in NZ?expand_more
Yes. Most licensed studios list the theoretical RTP in the game's info or paytable screen. Some operators run configurable versions at a lower RTP, so confirm the figure inside the game rather than trusting a review elsewhere.
Which has the best return — pokies or table games?expand_more
Blackjack with basic strategy (~99.5% RTP) and baccarat's banker bet (~98.9%) beat almost every pokie. European (single-zero) roulette returns about 97.3%. High-RTP pokies run 96–99% with no strategy required, which is why many Kiwis still prefer them.
What is the highest RTP pokie a Kiwi can play?expand_more
Book of 99 from Relax Gaming is one of the highest at 99.0% RTP, and Blood Suckers by NetEnt sits at 98.0% with low volatility. How does volatility affect a high payout pokie?expand_more
Volatility decides how the return arrives, not how much there is. A low-volatility pokie pays small wins often and suits a modest NZD bankroll; a high-volatility one pays rarely but big and can empty a balance quickly. Two pokies with the same 96% RTP can feel completely different because of variance — pair a high RTP with low or medium volatility if you want your money to last.
Can you trust the RTP figures a casino displays?expand_more
Trust the figure inside the game's info screen over any homepage badge. A payout rate is only genuinely verified when an independent lab — eCOGRA, iTech Labs or GLI — backs it with a dated report or a certificate number you can check on the auditor's register. A logo with no paperwork behind it is marketing, not proof.

For a recreational player, casual gambling winnings are generally not treated as taxable income in New Zealand — you keep what you win. This is general information, not tax advice; if you gamble as a business or on a professional scale your situation can differ, so check with Inland Revenue or an accountant.
