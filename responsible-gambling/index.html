Responsible Gambling: Help & Support Tools for NZ Players

Gambling should always be entertainment — never a way to make money or escape a problem. If it stops being fun, free and confidential help is available right now, 24 hours a day.

Gambling is entertainment, not a way to make money

Every pokie and casino game is built with a mathematical edge in favour of the house. Over time, the house always wins — that's how these games are designed. The right way to think about gambling is as paid entertainment, like a movie ticket or a night out: you pay for the fun, and you should never expect to profit. Treat any money you gamble as spent the moment you deposit it.

The moment gambling becomes a way to make money, pay bills, or win back what you've lost, it stops being safe. Chasing losses is the most common path into harm. If you find yourself betting more to recover, that's a signal to stop and reach out for help.

🔞 You must be 18 or older to gamble in New Zealand. Gambling can be harmful. Free confidential help: Gambling Helpline NZ 0800 654 655, or text 8006.

Safeguarding tools that keep you in control

Reputable sites give you tools to stay in control before things go wrong. Set them up when you sign up, not after you've had a bad session — decisions made calmly are always better than decisions made while chasing a loss. Look for these features in a site's account or "responsible gambling" settings:

💰 Deposit limits

Cap how much you can deposit per day, week or month. This is the single most effective tool — set it to money you can genuinely afford to lose and stick to it.

📉 Loss limits

Set a maximum you're willing to lose over a period. Once you hit it, the site stops you spending more, no matter what.

⏱ Session & time limits

Limit how long a single session can last. When time's up, you're logged out — a simple way to avoid losing track of the clock.

🔔 Reality checks

Regular on-screen reminders showing how long you've been playing and how much you've spent, so nothing creeps up on you.

⏸ Time-outs (cool-off)

Take a short break — 24 hours, a week, or a month — where your account is locked. Good for stepping back before things escalate.

🚫 Self-exclusion

Block yourself from an operator for a longer, fixed period (often 6 months to permanent). Use this if you need a firm barrier between you and gambling.

The signs of problem gambling

Gambling harm can build slowly and is easy to miss in yourself. Any of the following can be a warning sign that gambling is becoming a problem — for you or someone close to you:

  • Spending more money or time gambling than you planned, or than you can afford.
  • Chasing losses — betting more to try to win back what you've lost.
  • Gambling to escape stress, boredom, loneliness or low mood.
  • Borrowing money, selling things, or missing bills to keep gambling.
  • Lying to family or friends about how much you gamble, or hiding it.
  • Feeling restless, anxious or irritable when you try to cut down or stop.
  • Neglecting work, study, relationships or responsibilities because of gambling.
  • Feeling guilt, regret or shame after gambling — but doing it again anyway.

If any of these feel familiar, you're not alone, and help is free and confidential. Reaching out early makes a real difference.

A quick self-assessment

Take a moment to answer these honestly. They're based on the kinds of questions counsellors use to help people understand their gambling:

  • Have you ever felt you should cut down on your gambling?
  • Have people close to you been worried about your gambling?
  • Have you ever felt guilty about the way you gamble, or what happens when you gamble?
  • Have you ever gambled to win back money you've lost?
  • Have you ever borrowed money or sold anything to fund gambling?

If you answered "yes" to even one of these, it's worth talking to someone. This is not a diagnosis — it's a prompt to check in with yourself. Free, confidential and anonymous self-tests and support are available from the Problem Gambling Foundation of New Zealand, or you can call the Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655 and simply talk it through with no pressure.

If gambling is causing you or someone you know harm, help is available 24/7 — free and confidential. Call the Gambling Helpline NZ on 0800 654 655 or text 8006.

Keeping gambling away from under-18s

Gambling is strictly for adults aged 18 and over. It is illegal for anyone under 18 to gamble, and we support every measure that keeps minors away from gambling content. If children share your device, use parental control and content-filtering software to block gambling sites, and never leave a logged-in gambling account accessible.

Useful blocking and filtering tools include Gamban and GamCare's resources, alongside your device's built-in screen-time and content controls.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Gambling Helpline really free and confidential?expand_more
Yes. Gambling Helpline NZ on 0800 654 655 is free to call, available 24/7, and confidential. You can also text 8006. You don't have to give your name, and there's no pressure — you can simply talk things through.
How do I self-exclude from a gambling site?expand_more
Most reputable operators have a self-exclusion option in the account or "responsible gambling" settings, or you can ask their support team. You choose a period — often six months up to permanent. You can also block gambling transactions with many NZ banks and use device-level blocking software.
Can I get help for a family member's gambling?expand_more
Absolutely. Gambling Helpline NZ and the Problem Gambling Foundation both support the family and whānau of people who gamble. You can call 0800 654 655 to talk about someone you're worried about.
What's the most effective way to stay in control?expand_more
Set a deposit limit before you play, treat the money as the cost of entertainment, set a time limit, and never chase losses. If you can't stick to your limits, take a time-out or self-exclude, and reach out for free support.
