What a live casino actually is

A live casino sits between a pokie and a trip to SkyCity. Instead of a random number generator, a real human dealer runs a real table from a professional studio, streamed to your phone or laptop in HD video. You place bets through the on-screen interface, and you watch the cards dealt and the wheel spun in real time.

The appeal for a lot of Kiwis is trust and atmosphere. You can see the shoe being shuffled and the ball dropping, so there's no "is the software rigged?" doubt that some players feel about pokies. You can also chat to the dealer and, at some tables, other players. It's the closest thing to a land-based casino you can get from your own couch at 11pm.

The trade-off is pace and stakes. Live tables run at the speed of a real dealer, so you play fewer hands per hour than on a pokie, and minimum bets are usually higher. That slower pace is actually a plus for bankroll control — it's much harder to blow through $200 NZD in ten minutes at a live blackjack table than on a fast-spinning pokie.

How live casinos work behind the screen

Understanding the plumbing helps you trust what you're watching. A live dealer game is filmed in a purpose-built studio — a real room with a real table, several HD cameras, and a trained croupier who deals physical cards or spins a physical wheel. What makes it "online" is a layer of technology called Optical Character Recognition (OCR), which reads every card value and wheel result as it happens and turns it into live data on your screen.

That data is what settles your bet. When the dealer turns the queen of hearts, the OCR system registers it instantly, the interface updates, and payouts are calculated automatically. A pit boss and studio manager oversee every table, and each session is recorded, so disputes can be reviewed frame by frame. This is why live games feel more transparent than a pokie: nothing is hidden inside software you can't see.

On your side, all you need is a browser and a stable connection. You log in, open the live lobby, pick a table, and take a seat (or bet behind at a full table). Bets are placed during a short countdown window, the round plays out on camera, and your balance updates the moment the result lands. A text chat lets you talk to the dealer, who will often greet you by name and answer in real time — one of the small touches that makes live play feel social rather than solitary.

The live dealer games Kiwis play

Live catalogues split into two camps: classic table games, and the TV-style game shows that have exploded in popularity. Here's what each one is and who it suits.

🃏 Live blackjack

The best-return live game — around 99.5% RTP with correct basic strategy. You play against the dealer, aiming for 21. Look for tables with side bets you can ignore and a low minimum in NZD.

🎡 Live roulette

Stick to European (single-zero) wheels at about 97.3% RTP; avoid American double-zero tables. Lightning and other multiplier variants add big-win potential at a slightly lower base return.

🀄 Live baccarat

Simple, fast, and low-edge — the banker bet returns roughly 98.9%. Hugely popular for its "no decisions" simplicity; you're just betting on player, banker or tie.

🎉 Game shows

Crazy Time, Monopoly Live, Lightning Dice and similar. Bright, presenter-led, and cheap to play from around $0.10 NZD. Fun and high-variance, but a noticeably higher house edge than blackjack.

If pure return matters to you, blackjack and baccarat are the smart picks — see our high payout casinos guide for the full RTP comparison. If you're playing for the entertainment, the game shows are hard to beat.

Live casino house edge — the numbers that decide your session

Every live game has a built-in advantage for the house, expressed either as "house edge" (the casino's cut) or "RTP" (return to player). They're two sides of the same coin: a 1% house edge means a 99% RTP. Over a long session the edge is what quietly erodes your bankroll, so choosing low-edge games matters more than any betting system ever will. Here's how the main live games compare.

Live casino bonuses and wagering — the honest version

This is the part most sites gloss over, so we'll be blunt. The big welcome bonuses casinos advertise are built around pokies, not live tables. When it comes to clearing wagering requirements, live dealer games usually contribute only 10–20% — and are often excluded entirely. That means a bonus can look great in the ad and be almost useless if you only want to play live blackjack.

Here's a worked NZD example so the trap is obvious. Say you claim a bonus of $100 NZD with 35× wagering. On pokies at 100% contribution you'd need to bet:

  • Pokies (100% contribution): $100 × 35 = $3,500 NZD of wagering to clear the bonus.
  • Live games at 10% contribution: every $1 you bet only counts as 10c toward wagering. To clear the same $3,500 requirement you'd need to wager $35,000 NZD at live tables — ten times as much.
  • Live games excluded (0%): your live bets count for nothing. You could play live blackjack all night and the wagering bar wouldn't move an inch.

So before you claim any bonus with live casino in mind, check three lines in the terms: the live-game contribution percentage, whether live games are excluded from the offer entirely, and the max-bet rule while a bonus is active (breaching it can void winnings). If live games contribute 0% or 10%, treat the bonus as a pokies offer and play live with your own cleared cash instead. Getting the most out of live dealer play

  1. Test your connection first. Live streaming needs stable bandwidth. A dropped connection mid-hand at blackjack can auto-stand you on a bad total. Play on Wi-Fi or a strong mobile signal.
  2. Learn basic strategy for blackjack. It's a printable chart, not a talent. Playing it correctly turns blackjack into the best-value game in the live lobby, at roughly 99.5% RTP.
  3. Choose European roulette every time. The single-zero wheel returns about 97.3%; the American double-zero wheel halves that edge in the house's favour. There's no reason to play American.
  4. Skip most side bets. Perfect Pairs, 21+3 and similar look tempting but carry a much higher house edge. They're entertainment tax, not value.
