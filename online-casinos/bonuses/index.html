The casino bonuses Kiwis are offered

New Zealand players see some of the biggest headline bonuses in the world, because offshore sites compete hard for Kiwi deposits. But "100% up to $2,000" tells you almost nothing on its own — the type of bonus and its terms decide the real value. Here are the main types you'll meet.

🎁 Match deposit bonus

The classic welcome offer — the casino matches a percentage of your deposit, e.g. 100% up to $500 NZD. Deposit $500, play with $1,000. The wagering and max bet decide whether it's worth it.

🎰 Deposit + free spins

A match bonus bundled with free spins on a named pokie. Check whether the spins carry their own wagering and cashout cap separate from the cash part.

💸 No deposit bonus

Free spins or a small credit for registering, no deposit needed. Low risk but tightly capped — see our full no deposit casinos guide.

🔄 Reload & cashback

Ongoing offers for existing players — a smaller match on later deposits, or a percentage of losses returned. Usually fairer wagering than the welcome bonus.

Beyond the four headline types, you'll also see a few specialist offers worth knowing by name:

  • No-wagering bonuses: the fairest kind — any winnings are cash you can withdraw immediately, with no playthrough. Usually smaller, but there's no catch to clear. These are rare and worth grabbing when the terms check out.
  • High-roller bonuses: larger match percentages on bigger deposits for players staking more. Wagering is often the same multiplier, so the turnover is proportionally huge — do the maths before committing.
  • Minimum-deposit bonuses: offers you can trigger with a $1, $5 or $10 deposit, good for testing a site cheaply. The bonus and any max cashout are scaled down to match.
  • New casino bonuses: freshly launched sites often lead with an oversized welcome offer to attract Kiwis. Treat the generous headline with the same scrutiny — a new brand still has to pay.

Across all of them, the same handful of terms decide the value: wagering, max bet, game weighting, cashout cap and expiry. Get those right and the headline barely matters. The rest of this page works through each one — with the real NZD maths that most casino bonuses NZ round-ups leave out.

Wagering, worked out with real numbers

Wagering (also called playthrough or the rollover) is the number of times you must bet the bonus before winnings can be withdrawn. It's the single most important term, and a worked example makes it clear. Let's take a common offer: 100% match up to $200, with 35× wagering on the bonus.

StepFigureExplanation
Your deposit$200 NZDYou put in $200 of your own money.
Bonus received$200 NZDThe 100% match gives you a $200 bonus on top.
Total to play with$400 NZDDeposit plus bonus.
Wagering (35× bonus)$7,000 NZD$200 bonus × 35 = $7,000 in total bets required.
If wagered on deposit + bonus$14,000 NZDSome sites apply 35× to deposit AND bonus — always check which.

So a $200 bonus can mean cycling $7,000 through pokies before you touch a withdrawal — and up to $14,000 if the wagering applies to your deposit as well. That's not money you lose; it's money you have to bet, over and over, with the house edge nibbling each cycle. The lower the multiplier, the easier the clear:

  • 25×–35×: fair for pokies, genuinely clearable if you have time to play.
  • 40×–50×: hard work; only take it if the max bet and expiry are generous.
  • 60× and up: designed not to be cleared. Skip it and play with your own funds.

Whether the multiplier applies to the bonus only, or the deposit plus bonus, can double the required turnover. It's the first thing to check in any offer's terms — and it's often buried a few clicks deep.

The max-bet rule — the trap that voids winnings

This is the term that catches more Kiwi players than any other. While you're clearing wagering, most casinos cap how much you can stake per spin — usually $5 to $7.50 NZD. Bet above that limit even once, and the casino can void the entire bonus and every dollar of winnings from it. No warning, no partial credit.

It's easy to breach by accident. You're spinning a pokie at $2 a spin, you hit a good run, you bump the stake to $10 to press your luck — and you've just forfeited the bonus. The casino's system flags the oversized bet automatically, and support will point at the terms you agreed to. This is genuinely the most common reason a bonus-related payout gets refused, and it's entirely avoidable.

  1. Find the max bet before you start. It's in the bonus terms, usually stated as a per-spin or per-round cap in NZD.
  2. Set your stake below it and leave it. Don't adjust bet size mid-session while a bonus is active.
  3. Watch "bet all" and quick-stake buttons. One tap can push you over the cap on a high-denomination pokie.
  4. Remember it applies to the whole wagering period. Not just the first spin — every bet until wagering is cleared.

Game weighting — why the game you pick matters

Not every game contributes equally to wagering. "Game weighting" (or contribution) sets what percentage of your bets on each game counts toward clearing the requirement. For pokie-focused welcome bonuses, this usually favours pokies heavily.

Game typeTypical contributionWhat it means for clearing wagering
Pokies / slots100%Every $1 bet clears $1 of wagering — the fastest route.
Roulette10–20%A $1 bet clears only 10–20c; slow, and some sites exclude it entirely.
Blackjack5–10%Very slow to clear; often excluded from bonus play altogether.
Live dealer0–10%Frequently contributes nothing — check before switching to live tables.
Jackpot pokies0–100%Some sites exclude progressive jackpots from bonus wagering.

The practical lesson: on a pokie welcome bonus, clear the wagering on pokies. Switch to blackjack or roulette mid-clear and you'll barely move the needle, or worse, breach a "no table games during wagering" clause and void the bonus. If you prefer table games, look for a bonus written for them, or skip the bonus and play unrestricted with your own funds.

Free-spin value — what "100 free spins" is actually worth

Free spins sound generous, but "100 free spins" is meaningless until you know the value per spin and the wagering on the winnings. The spin value is usually fixed and low — often $0.10 to $0.20 NZD per spin — regardless of what the pokie normally costs to play. Here's the maths on a typical bundle.

StepFigureExplanation
Free spins100The headline number.

Total spin value$10 NZD100 × $0.10 — the real stake the free spins represent.

So "100 free spins" is really about $10 of stake that, after wagering and a cashout cap, is worth a few dollars in your pocket on an average run. That's not a reason to skip them — free spins are low-risk and occasionally land a good hit — but it is a reason to ignore the spin count in the headline and read the value, wagering and cap underneath it. A "50 spins, no wagering, $50 cap" offer beats "200 spins, 40× wagering, $50 cap" comfortably.

How the max cashout and wagering interact

The max-cashout cap is the term that quietly limits your upside, and it's most punishing on no-deposit and free-spin bonuses. It sets the most you can ever withdraw from a bonus, no matter how much you win — commonly $50 to $100 NZD. The trap is how it interacts with wagering: you can grind through the full playthrough and still lose most of a big win to the cap.

Worked example. You claim a no-deposit bonus, get lucky and build the balance to $400 NZD. The bonus carries 40× wagering and a $100 max cashout:

  • Wagering: if the bonus was $20, that's $20 × 40 = $800 NZD you must turn over before any withdrawal.
  • You clear it and your balance is still $400 — a genuinely good result from a free bonus.
  • The $100 cap applies: you can withdraw $100. The other $300 is forfeited when the bonus completes.

That's the maths casinos rely on you not doing: the wagering makes you play a long time, and the cap makes sure the house keeps most of any big win. A deposit match bonus usually has a higher or no cashout cap on the deposit portion, which is one reason a matched deposit can be better value than a no-deposit freebie despite the smaller "free" headline. Always find the max cashout before you decide an offer is worth clearing.

How to claim a bonus, and how expiry works

Claiming is usually simple, but the order of steps matters — miss one and the bonus may not attach to your deposit.

  1. Register and verify. Open your account and complete KYC early. An unverified account is the number-one cause of a delayed first withdrawal, bonus or not.
  2. Opt in or enter the code. Some offers apply automatically; others need a bonus code or a tick-box at the cashier. Enter it before you deposit, not after.
  3. Meet the minimum deposit. Match bonuses have a qualifying minimum (often $10–$20 NZD); deposit at least that, and note some sites exclude certain methods like e-wallets from the offer.
  4. Play within the rules. Stay under the max bet, on weighted games, until wagering is cleared.
  5. Withdraw once cleared. When the wagering hits zero and any cap is respected, the balance becomes withdrawable in NZD.

Expiry — the deadline that makes or breaks an offer

Every bonus has an expiry, typically 7 to 30 days from claiming, sometimes as little as 24 hours on free spins. If you don't clear the wagering in that window, the bonus and any winnings tied to it are removed. Cross-check the expiry against your worked clear time: if a bonus needs 40 hours of play but expires in 7 days, you're committing to nearly six hours a day — a sign the offer isn't built to be beaten. A fair bonus gives you enough time to clear it at a relaxed pace.

Is a casino bonus worth claiming?

Not always — and being able to say no is what protects your bankroll. A bonus is a marketing tool designed to keep you playing longer, so weigh it honestly.

👍 When it's worth it

Low, bonus-only wagering (25×–35×), a workable max bet, pokies weighted 100%, a fair cashout cap and enough expiry to clear at a relaxed pace. No-wagering offers are the clearest win of all.

👎 When to skip it

60×+ wagering, a $1–$2 max bet, deposit-plus-bonus turnover, a tiny cashout cap, or an expiry too short for the maths. Playing with your own funds gives more freedom and faster withdrawals.

The advantage of a good bonus is simple: more playing time and more chances to hit a win from the same deposit. The disadvantage is equally simple: your money is locked to the wagering rules until it clears, so you can't withdraw mid-way, and you can be tempted to bet more than you planned. If an offer only works by pushing you past your budget, it isn't worth it — full stop.

How to read an offer in two minutes

  1. Wagering multiplier and base. Is it 35× the bonus, or 35× deposit + bonus? Lower and bonus-only is better.
  2. Max bet while wagering. $5–$7.50 NZD is standard. Note it and stay under it.
  3. Game weighting. Confirm pokies count 100% and check for excluded games.
  4. Max cashout. Especially on free-spin and no-deposit portions — often capped at $50–$100 NZD.
  5. Expiry. 7–30 days is typical. If you can't clear the wagering in time, it's worthless.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good casino welcome bonus for NZ players?expand_more
Judge it by the terms, not the headline. For pokies, look for wagering of 35× or lower, a max bet around $5–$7.50 NZD, pokies weighted at 100%, and a reasonable cashout cap. A "100% up to $2,000" offer at 60× wagering is worse than a smaller bonus at 30×.
How does bonus wagering work?expand_more
Wagering is how many times you must bet the bonus (and sometimes the deposit) before withdrawing. A $100 bonus at 35× means placing $3,500 in bets first. Pokies usually count 100% toward this; table games count 10% or less.
What is the max bet rule on a bonus?expand_more
It caps how much you can stake per spin while clearing wagering, usually $5–$7.50 NZD. Breaching it — even once — lets the casino void the bonus and its winnings. It's the most common reason a bonus payout is refused, so check it first.
Should I always take the welcome bonus?expand_more
No. If the wagering is high, the max bet is tiny, or the expiry is too short to clear realistically, skipping the bonus and playing with your own funds gives more freedom and faster withdrawals. Take a bonus only when the terms make it genuinely clearable.
Does wagering apply to my deposit too?expand_more
It depends on the site. Some apply the multiplier to the bonus only; others to deposit plus bonus, which roughly doubles the turnover required. Always check the base the wagering applies to — it's the biggest hidden variable in an offer's real value.
Can I withdraw bonus funds?expand_more
Not directly. Bonus money itself can't be cashed out — you withdraw the winnings you make with it, and only once you've met the wagering requirement and stayed within the max-bet and max-cashout rules. Any bonus balance above the cashout cap is forfeited when the bonus completes.
Are no deposit casino bonuses real?expand_more
Yes, they're real, but tightly capped. A no-deposit bonus gives you free spins or a small credit just for registering, with no deposit needed. Expect high wagering (often 40×+) and a low max cashout of $50–$100 NZD, so it's a low-risk way to try a site rather than a route to a big win. See our no-deposit casinos guide.
What is a no-wagering casino bonus?expand_more
A no-wagering (or wager-free) bonus means any winnings are cash you can withdraw straight away, with no playthrough to clear. It's the fairest bonus type. Offers are usually smaller than headline match bonuses, but there's no catch — which often makes them better real value than a big bonus at 40×+ wagering.
Can I claim a casino bonus on mobile?expand_more
Yes. At the top sites the whole cashier — deposits, bonus codes, opt-ins and KYC uploads — works in a mobile browser, so you can claim and clear a bonus entirely from your phone. Your bonus progress and wagering carry across if you switch to a laptop, because it's the same account.
